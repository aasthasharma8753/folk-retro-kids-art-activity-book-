# Commercial Tier Strategy — Kalighat Reframe

Reference: `docs/ART_DIRECTION.md` for the physical innovations this tiering is built around. The stack in §3–4 of the creative bible (deboss, scent ink, magnetic shadow-box cover) is expensive and copyable in pieces but not as a whole — the tier strategy exists to protect margin on the full-stack version while still giving copycats no cheap door in at the low end.

## The Three Tiers

| Tier | Name | Physical Stack | Target Buyer | Strategic Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Heirloom Edition** | Full stack: 400gsm cotton pulp with deep Topographic Channel deboss + micro-encapsulated scent ink + Swiss-bound magnetic shadow-box cover | Gifting occasion (baby's first Diwali, heritage-conscious grandparent gift), design-press/PR sample | Halo product — this is what gets written up, photographed, and gifted. Margin secondary to brand-defining showpiece status. |
| **2** | **Studio Edition** | Deboss channels + magnetic shadow-box cover, **standard non-scented soy-based ink** (drop the microcapsule cost driver) | Core diaspora parent buying for their own toddler, repeat-purchase household | Volume driver — keeps the ritual (§5) and the tactile deboss intact, which is the emotional core; cuts the single most expensive, least load-bearing feature (scent). |
| **3** | **Discovery Edition** | Flat-printed scroll (no deboss), simple hardcover **without** the fold-back frame mechanic, same Kalighat art system | Retail sampler, gift-with-purchase, price-sensitive first-time buyer, museum-shop/boutique impulse buy | Gateway product — introduces the art system and roster at accessible cost, builds demand for a Tier 1/2 upgrade without ever touching the Heirloom tooling investment. |

## Why This Tiering Is Defensible, Not Just a Discount Ladder

- A copycat can flat-print the Kalighat art style relatively fast (Tier 3 is inherently the most exposed tier — treat it as a loss-leader/marketing tool, not a margin product).
- **Tier 2's deboss + magnetic frame is the real moat**: it requires the specific paper vendor relationship and magnet-hinge tooling from Phase 1 of `docs/PRODUCT_ROADMAP.md`. A competitor copying only the art gets a flat coloring book — they don't get the ritual.
- **Tier 1's scent ink is the hardest and most expensive layer to clone** (microencapsulation sourcing + child-safety certification is a multi-month, multi-vendor process) — it should never be discounted into Tier 2 or 3 as a way to move inventory; keep it exclusive to Heirloom or it stops signaling "the one they can't copy."

## Sequencing Note

Do not launch all three tiers simultaneously. Recommended order:

1. **Heirloom Edition first**, in a limited run — this is the press/gifting story and the one that proves the ritual works with real families (Phase 4 of the roadmap).
2. **Studio Edition second**, once deboss + magnetic cover manufacturing is de-risked at Heirloom volume.
3. **Discovery Edition last**, once the roster and art system have market validation — releasing it too early risks anchoring the brand as "just a coloring book" before the Heirloom halo effect has landed.

## Open Questions for Pricing (flag for a follow-up commercial pass, not answered here)

- Target retail price bands per tier, and whether Heirloom is positioned as a limited numbered edition (further defensibility + gifting justification).
- Whether Tier 3 carries any of the roster's scent/deboss story in marketing copy even without the physical feature, to upsell toward Tier 1/2.
- Direct-to-consumer vs. boutique/museum-shop wholesale margin structure, given Tier 1's higher COGS from certification and low-volume tooling.
